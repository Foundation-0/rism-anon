# Scouting notes, 18–19 October 2026

This is the record of the work done before the repository was set up: Foundation0's scouting of 18–19 October 2026
([deep-fields](https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/tree/main/deep-fields)) and a pilot run on 19 October 2026.

## The question

Which anonymous music manuscripts in RISM open with the same melody as a work RISM attributes to a named composer?

RISM (Répertoire International des Sources Musicales) catalogues music manuscripts and early prints in libraries
worldwide, and records the opening bars of each piece as an "incipit". Many manuscripts are anonymous. If an
anonymous copy opens with the same notes as a piece RISM credits to a named composer, it may be a copy of that piece.
RISM's editors decide.

## Checked on 19 Oct 2026

### RISM's data

- RISM publishes a monthly export of its records. RISM's news post of 30 Oct 2025
  ([link](https://rism.info/new_at_rism/2025/10/30/new-webpage-for-rism-data-exports.html)) says: "The RISM dataset
  is released as open data with a Creative Commons CC-BY 3.0 license", and that the exports are updated at the start
  of each month, in MARCXML. The scouting report said CC BY. The version, 3.0, was checked during setup.
- The file list is at https://rism.digital/exports/files.json. On 19 Oct 2026 it gave the source export of
  1 Oct 2026 as `source-latest.xml.gz`, 542 MB, SHA-1 `c07ba3fb5cfb535af155956ffe97b51eca2a63e6`. The copy used by
  the pilot has that SHA-1. Older exports are under `https://rism.digital/exports/archive/`. That folder has no
  index page, but files.json lists them.
- That export holds 1,626,011 source records, 296,509 of them anonymous. The scouting report gave these numbers,
  and the pilot counted them in the export.

### The pilot

Scripts: [pilot/match.py](../pilot/match.py) and [pilot/match_genre.py](../pilot/match_genre.py). Counts:
[results/pilot-2026-10-19.md](../results/pilot-2026-10-19.md).

- Rule: an exact match on the first 12 pitches, with at least 5 different pitches, skipping anonymous records that
  already cite a thematic catalogue.
- 138,817 anonymous records have a usable incipit. 13,696 match attributed records of exactly one composer.
- Only 186 of those fall in the four genres the other project covers (symphonies, concertos, operas, overtures).
- About 4,000 (3,980) are chorales, sacred songs and chorale arrangements, where shared tunes are expected and
  matches are low value.
- Others include 682 keyboard pieces, 477 masses, 419 arias and 345 motets.
- A match does not settle who wrote the music. It may be an arrangement, a new text set to the same tune, or a wrong
  attribution on the named copy.
- During setup, match_genre.py was re-run on the same export. It took about 5 minutes and gave output identical to
  the pilot's, apart from the printing order of two genres tied at 108.

## From the scouting report, not yet re-checked

- RISM says it aims to reduce anonymous sources by matching incipits (RISM blog, April 2024,
  https://rism.info/new_at_rism/2024/04/25/correcting-errors-in-risms-music-incipits.html).
- RISM is building a works authority, a catalogue of musical works, with incipits (Pugin et al., poster, Oxford
  2025, https://dlfm.web.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/dlfm/documents/media/2025-poster-pugin.pdf).
- Another project, aaymeloglu/rism-attributions (https://github.com/aaymeloglu/rism-attributions), run with Claude
  and Codex and last updated 3 Oct 2026, covers symphonies, concertos, operas and overtures: 3,877 anonymous sources
  searched, 213 judged "same music". It had reported nothing to RISM yet. Under f0's policy for other people's
  projects (https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md), this project contacts it first to split the
  work, and credits it.
